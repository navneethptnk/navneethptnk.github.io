Investigation 01 — Data Quality / Reporting

Can we trust the data before we trust the dashboard?

Context

A trading desk relied on daily reports built from a live trade feed. Reports were being trusted at face value, without a clear picture of the feed's underlying quality.

Question

Before improving the dashboard, could the numbers behind it actually be trusted?

Data

Raw trade records including timestamps, instrument identifiers, volumes and counterparties, pulled directly from the source feed with no prior cleaning applied.

Validation

Ran systematic checks across the feed to separate genuine activity from noise introduced by the pipeline itself.

Observation

Trade volume increased, but the reporting dataset contained duplicate records.

Why it mattered

Raw volume would have overstated activity, distorting any decision made from the report.

Analysis

Used Python and SQL to profile missingness and duplication rates, reconcile records against source counts, and isolate the specific pipeline stage introducing the errors.

Findings

Roughly a quarter of the reported volume swing on high-activity days traced back to duplicate entries rather than genuine trading, concentrated around a specific ingestion window.

Decision

Built automated checks for missing and duplicate records ahead of reporting, improving data reliability by 25% and cutting roughly 15 hours per week of manual reconciliation work.

Action

Added automated duplicate validation before reporting, with the Power BI dashboard rebuilt on top of the validated dataset as the final communication layer.

Investigation 02 — Business Analytics / Forecasting

What makes one route worth expanding?

Context

An airline network spanning many routes needed a clearer, data-backed view of where performance was strongest and where expansion made sense.

Question

Which factors actually separated a high-performing route from an underperforming one?

Data

500K+ records spanning route-level operational and performance data, extracted and consolidated from multiple source tables.

Validation

Checked for inconsistent route naming, seasonal gaps and outlier records before drawing any conclusions about performance.

Analysis

Cleaned and transformed the dataset with Python and Pandas, used SQL to aggregate performance by route and period, then applied forecasting to project how performance drivers might hold going forward.

Findings

A small set of routes consistently accounted for a disproportionate share of network performance, with load factor and turnaround time emerging as the clearest recurring drivers.

Decision

Findings were packaged into a Power BI view highlighting the strongest expansion candidates, giving stakeholders a defensible starting point for route-planning discussions.

Investigation 03 — Statistics / Strategy

Where should the next store go?

Context

A retail expansion decision needed to move beyond intuition toward a measurable, comparable score across candidate locations.

Question

Which combination of local factors best predicted whether a location would perform well?

Data

Demographic datasets and competitor location data for each candidate site, joined and prepared using SQL.

Validation

Checked for correlated features and inconsistent geographic granularity before modelling, so the regression wasn't misled by redundant variables.

Analysis

Engineered features describing demographics, competition and accessibility, then applied multiple regression modelling to relate those features to expected site performance.

Findings

Demographic density and distance from the nearest competitor together explained the majority of variation in modelled site performance, more than any single factor alone.

Decision

Converted the regression output into a simple comparable score per candidate site, turning a subjective location debate into a measurable, data-backed decision.
